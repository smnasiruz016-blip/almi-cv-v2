Most countries' CV conventions are matters of taste — page counts, photos, fonts. Poland's
defining convention is a legal sentence. Zielona Linia, the Polish government's own careers
service, puts it plainly: without the data-processing consent clause at the bottom of your CV,
**a potential employer cannot even phone you back.**

## The failure

The failure mode is silence. You send a strong Polish CV — right experience, tidy layout — and
nothing happens, because the last line is missing. Zielona Linia's CV guide warns:

> "Pamiętaj, by umieścić tę klauzulę; bez niej potencjalny pracodawca nie może nawet do Ciebie
> zadzwonić."
> — *"Remember to include this clause; without it, a potential employer cannot even call you."*

This is not recruiter folklore. It follows from RODO — the Polish name for the GDPR — and the
government service spells out the mechanism:

> "Rekruter ma prawo korzystać z naszego CV i informacji tam zawartych tylko wtedy, gdy pisemnie
> wyrazimy na to zgodę."
> — *"A recruiter has the right to use our CV and the information contained in it only when we
> give written consent to it."*

No consent, no lawful processing. No lawful processing, no phone call.

## The clause itself

**What it is:** your written consent for the employer to process the personal data in your CV
for recruitment purposes.

**Where it goes:** at the very end of the CV — the footer, after everything else, usually in a
smaller font.

**The current wording**, per Zielona Linia:

> "Wyrażam zgodę na przetwarzanie moich danych osobowych przez (nazwa firmy) w celu prowadzenia
> rekrutacji"
> — *"I consent to the processing of my personal data by (company name) for the purpose of
> conducting recruitment."*

Replace *(nazwa firmy)* with the actual company name. Zielona Linia also gives variants that
cite RODO explicitly; the version above is the current core wording. Many employers publish
their own preferred clause in the job posting — **if the posting supplies its own wording, use
that wording.**

### Worked example: the same footer, with and without

*Illustration — not a real application:*

**Without the clause:**

```
Języki: polski (ojczysty), angielski (C1)
Prawo jazdy kat. B
```

The CV simply ends. Per the government guidance quoted above, the recruiter now holds personal
data they have no written consent to use — and cannot even ring you.

**With the clause:**

```
Języki: polski (ojczysty), angielski (C1)
Prawo jazdy kat. B

Wyrażam zgodę na przetwarzanie moich danych osobowych przez Softwarehouse Sp. z o.o.
w celu prowadzenia rekrutacji.
```

One sentence, small font, last line. That is the entire difference.

## The other Polish conventions

Common in Poland — and, as always, the posting or the employer's own application form outranks
any general guide, including this one.

| Field | Common in Poland | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Page length | Flexible — one page where possible, two if needed | Zielona Linia; Pracuj.pl |
| Photo | Optional — your choice, not a requirement | Zielona Linia |
| Address | City is enough; full street address not required | InterviewMe.pl |
| Date of birth | Not required — safe to leave off | Pracuj.pl; InterviewMe.pl |
| GPA / grades | No Polish guidance found — regional default: leave off | (see below) |
| References | "Available on request" line, not referee details | WUP Katowice |

**Page length.** Pracuj.pl: "Na to, ile stron powinno mieć CV, nie ma złotej reguły" — *"there
is no golden rule for how many pages a CV should have."* One page for early careers, two is
acceptable for senior experience. Zielona Linia advises fitting one page where you can — and,
memorably, not turning the page sideways to force it: "nie ustawiaj strony poziomo, jeżeli dane
nie mieszczą się na jednej kartce" (*"don't set the page landscape if the data doesn't fit on
one sheet"*).

**Photo.** Customary for many applicants, but Zielona Linia is blunt about the law: "W Kodeksie
pracy nie ma wzmianki o tym, że zdjęcie w życiorysie jest wymagane, dlatego żądanie przyszłego
pracodawcy w tym zakresie nie będzie zgodne z przepisami" — *"the Labour Code makes no mention
of a photo being required, so a prospective employer's demand for one would not comply with the
law."* Include one or don't; it is your call.

**Date of birth.** Pracuj.pl: "nie jest to konieczne" — *"it is not necessary."* InterviewMe.pl
agrees: "Nie masz obowiązku podawania daty urodzenia w CV" — *"you have no obligation to give
your date of birth in a CV."*

**References.** WUP Katowice (a voivodeship labour office) advises that "należy wskazać w CV, że
referencje są możliwe do uzyskania na życzenie" — *"indicate in the CV that references can be
obtained on request"* — and advises against handing out referees' contact details.

## What we could not verify

Honesty about source strength, field by field:

- **GPA/grades:** no Polish guidance found at all — not in Zielona Linia, the voivodeship labour
  offices, or the platforms consulted. "Leave it off" here is a **regional default**, not a
  verified Polish convention.
- **Address:** the weakest sourced field — it rests on InterviewMe.pl, a CV-advice platform, not
  a government source.
- Everything else above traces to Poland's government employment services (zielonalinia.gov.pl,
  wupkatowice.praca.gov.pl) or major Polish job platforms, quoted verbatim.

And the standing caveat: **a specific job posting, or an employer's own application form with its
own consent checkbox, outranks this page.** If the employer's portal collects consent itself,
follow the portal.

## What actually costs you

- **Omitting the clause entirely** — per the government's own careers service, the recruiter
  cannot lawfully act on your CV.
- **Leaving "(nazwa firmy)" unfilled** — a template placeholder in a legal sentence signals a
  mass mailing.
- **Ignoring the posting's own clause** — when the employer specifies wording, theirs wins.
- **Padding the CV with data Poland doesn't ask for** — full street address, date of birth —
  personal data with no upside.
- **Forcing one page by contortion** — Polish sources allow two pages; sideways layouts and
  micro-fonts help nobody.
