The Swiss Lebenslauf runs opposite to anglophone advice on almost every line. The University of
Zurich's career service asks for a full postal address plus Geburtsdatum and Geburtsort — date
and place of birth — in the header, jobs.ch calls a photo "nach wie vor üblich" (still customary),
and the federal employment service allows up to three pages. **The same header a London recruiter
would question is standard in Zürich.**

## The failure

The mistake this page prevents is symmetrical. Candidates trained on UK or US advice send a
stripped one-pager into a Swiss process and read as withholding — no photo, no birth date, a
city-only address, where the local convention expects all three. Candidates trained on Swiss
convention do the reverse abroad and get flagged for exactly the data they were taught to include.

**Neither header is wrong. Each is wrong in the other country.** This page covers what Swiss
sources actually say, field by field, with the source for each claim.

## The conventions, field by field

Everything below comes from three in-country sources: SECO (the federal government's
arbeit.swiss application tips), UZH Career Services (university career centre), and jobs.ch
(established local job platform, used here as corroboration).

| Field | Common in Switzerland | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 2–3 pages accepted | SECO; UZH |
| Photo | Customary, not compulsory | jobs.ch; UZH |
| Address | Full postal address | UZH |
| Date/place of birth | Expected in personal details | UZH |
| Grades | Include if good or excellent | UZH |
| References | Withheld until requested | UZH; SECO |
| Marital status, children | Optional (fakultativ) | UZH |

**Length — flexible, up to three pages.** SECO's tips say the CV

> "should be no longer than three pages and structured chronologically, with your most recent
> job mentioned first."

UZH agrees: "das Dokument sollte nicht mehr als zwei bis drei Seiten umfassen" — the document
should run no more than two to three pages. Neither the strict one-page norm of the US nor the
two-page norm of the UK applies.

**Photo — still customary, with a visible counter-trend.** jobs.ch:

> "In der Schweiz ist es im Gegensatz zu Ländern wie den USA nach wie vor üblich, dass ein
> Lebenslauf ein Foto enthält." — In Switzerland, unlike countries such as the USA, it is still
> customary for a CV to contain a photo.

The same jobs.ch article notes: "Vereinzelt gibt es neuerdings jedoch auch die Tendenz, dass
grosse Unternehmen bewusst darauf hinweisen, sich auch ohne Foto zu bewerben" — occasionally
there is now a tendency for large companies to explicitly invite applications without a photo.
UZH treats the photo as not mandatory unless the posting asks for it, but says it makes the
application more personal — professional, head-and-shoulders, no more than two years old.

**Address — full, unabbreviated.** UZH asks for the "vollständige Adresse ohne Abkürzungen wie
str." — the complete address, with no abbreviations such as "str." for Strasse.

**Date and place of birth — expected.** UZH lists "Geburtsdatum und Geburtsort" among the
personal details to state. Marital status and children are explicitly "fakultativ" — optional.

**Grades — include when they help.** UZH advises including final grades "wenn gut oder
hervorragend" — if good or excellent. Otherwise leave them off.

**References — held back until asked.** UZH is unusually direct:

> "Es gibt ein ungeschriebenes Gesetz, das besagt, dass Referenzen nicht im CV aufgeführt
> werden, wenn dies nicht ausdrücklich in der Stellenausschreibung gefordert wird." — There is
> an unwritten law that references are not listed in the CV unless the job posting expressly
> asks for them.

SECO matches: "personal references will be provided upon request" — a separate reference sheet
may be handed over at interview. So the Swiss CV is fuller than the anglophone one on personal
data, yet on references it lands where Harvard-style advice does: not on the page.

## Worked example: the same header, two countries

Illustration only — not a real person.

A UK-style minimal header:

```
Anna Keller
Zürich · anna.keller@example.ch · +41 79 000 00 00
```

The same candidate, Swiss-style:

```
Anna Keller
Musterstrasse 12, 8004 Zürich
+41 79 000 00 00 · anna.keller@example.ch
Geboren am 14.03.1995 in Luzern
[professional photo, top right]
```

What changed, and why: the city becomes a full street address, unabbreviated, because UZH asks
for the "vollständige Adresse" — and note it is written out as Musterstrasse, not Musterstr.
A birth line appears because UZH lists Geburtsdatum and Geburtsort among expected personal
details. A photo slot appears because jobs.ch reports one is still customary. Nothing else
about the CV needs to change — the body sections carry the same evidence either way. **The
delta between the two conventions is almost entirely in the header.**

## What is settled, and what is not

The claims above do not all rest on equal ground, so weigh them accordingly.

- **Government-backed:** the three-page ceiling and references-on-request come from SECO, the
  federal employment service — the strongest source type on this page.
- **University career centre:** full address, birth details, grades-if-good, and the unwritten
  law on references come from UZH Career Services — authoritative for how Swiss applications
  are read, but one institution's guide, not regulation.
- **Platform corroboration:** the photo claim rests on jobs.ch, a commercial platform. It is
  corroboration-grade, and the same source records large companies starting to invite
  photo-free applications — this convention is visibly in motion.

One nuance the sources carry: the photo evidence speaks to the German-speaking market
specifically. Our sources do not separately document convention in Romandie or Ticino, so we
will not claim it is identical there.

**And conventions shift — the posting outranks this page.** If a Swiss employer's advert or
careers site says apply without a photo, or asks for referees up front, that instruction wins
over every line above. When in doubt, check with the target employer.

## What actually costs you

- An abbreviated or city-only address where UZH expects the full, unabbreviated one.
- Referees listed on the CV when the posting never asked — against UZH's "unwritten law".
- A mediocre grade included; UZH says include grades only when good or excellent.
- A dated or casual photo — worse than none; UZH's bar is professional and under two years old.
- Sending the fuller Swiss header, unchanged, to a UK or US employer — see the related pages.
