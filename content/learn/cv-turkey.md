Most CV advice on the internet is written in English for the US and UK — where photos are out,
birth dates are out, and references are "available on request". Turkish convention sits closer
to continental Europe than to any of that. **Kariyer.net, Turkey's major job platform, tells
candidates their birth date "mutlaka yazılmalı" — must definitely be written — and to add
photos "taken in a suit."** Follow the anglophone stripped-down formula and your özgeçmiş
reads under-dressed in Istanbul.

## The failure

The mistake this page prevents: applying in Turkey with a CV built to anglophone rules —
no photo, no birth date, no address, "references available on request" — because that is what
most search results teach.

None of that is wrong in London. In Turkey, the sources point the other way on almost every
field. The reverse failure exists too: carrying a Turkish-convention CV — photo, doğum tarihi,
marital status — into an international application, where the same Turkish sources say to
strip it out.

## What Turkish sources actually say

Convention by field, each with its source. These are conventions, not rules — "Common in
Turkey" is the honest frame throughout.

| Field | Common in Turkey | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 2 pages maximum; academic CVs may run longer | Kariyer.net: "Özgeçmişin en fazla 2 sayfa olmalı" |
| Photo | Customary — formal, suit, clean background | Kariyer.net, Resume-Example |
| Address | Included, traditionally the full posta adresi | Kariyer.net |
| Birth date | Commonly included | Kariyer.net |
| GPA | Optional — worth adding if strong | Kariyer.net, StylingCV |
| References | About two, named on the CV | Kariyer.net, Resume-Example |

The quotes behind the table:

> "Adresini, sana ulaşılabilecek telefon numaralarını ve e-mail adresini özgeçmişinin başına
> mutlaka yazmalısın." — Kariyer.net
> [You must write your address, reachable phone numbers and email at the top of your CV.]

> "Doğum tarihi, doğum yeri gibi bilgiler mutlaka yazılmalı." — Kariyer.net
> [Information such as date of birth and place of birth must definitely be written.]

> "Okurken aldığın dereceleri ya da önemli başarıları ve genel not ortalamasını
> belirtebilirsin." — Kariyer.net
> [You may state degrees, notable achievements and your GPA.] StylingCV narrows it: include
> GPA "3.00+ ise" — if it is 3.00 or above.

On references, Turkish sources are warmer than almost any anglophone one:

> "Turkish employers may place more emphasis on references than employers in other countries.
> If possible, include a reference from a previous Turkish employer." — Resume-Example

Kariyer.net corroborates: "İki referans yazılabilir" [two references may be written] — and
adds a courtesy the anglophone guides share: "referans gösterdiğin kişilere önceden haber
vermelisin" [notify your referees in advance].

## The photo: customary, not mandatory

Worth being precise here, because the sources are. Resume-Example says "In Turkey, it's
common to have a photo on your CV" and advises a recent, professional-looking photograph at
the top. Kariyer.net gets specific about what kind:

> "Takım elbiseli çekilmiş fotoğraflar eklemelisin." — Kariyer.net
> [You should add photos taken in a suit.] And the negative: "Silik, bulanık, arka planı
> karışık fotoğraflar eklememelisin" — no faded, blurry, or cluttered-background photos.

**But no source in our fact base calls the photo mandatory — so we map it "optional",
customary and advantageous.** One CV-builder site circulates a percentage for how much a photo
helps; it publishes no methodology, so this page does not repeat the number. The honest reading:
expected in practice, formal in style, not a written requirement.

## Worked example: two headers

An illustration, not a real person. First, the header the anglophone internet teaches:

```
Aylin Demir
Istanbul · aylin.demir@example.com · +90 5xx xxx xx xx
```

Clean, and correct for a London application. Now the same candidate to Turkish convention:

```
[Formal photo — suit, plain background]
Aylin Demir
Adres: Kadıköy, İstanbul (posta adresi)
Telefon: +90 5xx xxx xx xx · E-posta: aylin.demir@example.com
Doğum tarihi: 1994, İstanbul

Referanslar (CV sonunda):
Ad Soyad — Genel Müdür, [önceki işveren] — telefon, e-posta
Ad Soyad — Takım Lideri, [önceki işveren] — telefon, e-posta
```

Line by line: the photo satisfies the suit-photo convention Kariyer.net describes. The
address answers its "mutlaka yazmalısın" instruction — traditionally the full postal
address, though see the caveat below. Doğum tarihi answers "mutlaka yazılmalı". The two
named referees, with contact details and advance warning, follow "iki referans yazılabilir".
Everything the first header omits is something a Turkish source explicitly asks for.

One boundary the sources themselves draw: StylingCV warns that on an international CV,
"yaş, medeni hal, doğum yeri — uluslararası CV'de ayrımcılık sayılır" — age, marital status
and birthplace count as discrimination risks abroad. The Turkish-convention header is for
Turkish employers; do not export it.

## What is not settled

- **Source strength.** We could not locate İŞKUR (government employment service) guidance
  for this page. The fact base rests on Kariyer.net — the major local platform — corroborating
  two independent Turkish-market guides on every field. That is platform corroboration, not
  official guidance; a government source could shift details.
- **Address granularity.** Sources say include an address but never specify how precise.
  "Full postal address" reflects the traditional posta adresi convention; modern practice
  trends toward district plus city, and a stronger source could downgrade this.
- **The photo.** Customary and formal per every source — but none calls it mandatory.
- **Conventions move.** Guides published in 2026 already hedge for international
  applications; domestic norms can follow. **If the job posting or the employer's portal
  says something different from this page, the posting wins.** When in doubt, check with
  the target employer.

## What actually costs you

- A photo-less, detail-stripped anglophone CV that reads under-dressed next to local
  candidates who followed local convention.
- A casual or cluttered photo — Kariyer.net rejects "silik, bulanık" photos explicitly;
  a bad photo is worse than the debate about having one.
- "References available on request" where two named referees are the norm — and referees
  who get a surprise call because you never warned them.
- Three pages. Even with 20 years' experience, StylingCV says, "2 sayfayı geçme" — do not
  exceed two.
- Exporting the Turkish header abroad: birth date and marital status on an international
  CV are flagged by Turkish sources themselves.
