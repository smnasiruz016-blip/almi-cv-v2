The single line most international CV guides tell you to write — "References available on
request" — is the line Nigerian guidance singles out for dislike. Jobberman, one of Nigeria's
largest job platforms, lists it among the details recruiters do not want to see.
**A CV polished to London rules arrives in Lagos looking like it is hiding something.**

## The failure

The failure is applying the UK or US reference convention to a Nigerian application. In those
markets, withholding referees until asked is standard, even polite. In Nigeria, the weight of
local guidance runs the other way: referees are named, with contact details, on the CV itself.

> "Most Nigerian employers request two to three referees." / "Recruiters dislike irrelevant
> details such as … 'references available on request.'"
> — Jobberman Nigeria

A candidate who omits the referee section has not looked tidy and modern. To a reader expecting
named referees, the omission can read as evasion — as if there is no lecturer, no former
employer, no professional contact willing to vouch for you.

## The referee section, in detail

The local guidance is specific about who and how many.

> "You need to provide at least two references. One from your academic life (a lecturer or your
> project supervisor) and the other can be your former employer."
> — NigerianQueries CV guide

Jobberman corroborates the count: **two to three referees is what most Nigerian employers
request.** So the section looks like this in practice:

- **How many:** two at minimum, three at most.
- **Who:** a mix — an academic referee (lecturer or project supervisor) plus a professional
  one (a former employer or manager). For experienced hires, two professional referees is a
  natural fit.
- **What details:** name, role and organisation, and a working contact — the point of the
  convention is that the recruiter can actually reach them.

One honest caveat, because the sources do not fully agree: MyCVCreator's Nigeria guide notes
that "many candidates simply write 'References available on request'". That tells you the
on-request habit exists in the wild. But the local-platform and local-guide weight — Jobberman
and NigerianQueries — favours naming referees, and Jobberman explicitly reports recruiter
distaste for the on-request line. We report both; the named-referee convention is the safer
default for Nigerian employers.

## Worked example: the same block, two ways

*The names below are illustrative — this is a format comparison, not a real CV.*

**The on-request way (UK-polished):**

```
References available on request.
```

**The Nigerian-convention way:**

```
REFEREES

Dr. A. Okonkwo — Senior Lecturer, Department of Economics, University of Lagos
   a.okonkwo@example.edu.ng · +234 80X XXX XXXX

Mrs. F. Adeyemi — Operations Manager, Example Logistics Ltd (former line manager)
   f.adeyemi@example.com · +234 70X XXX XXXX
```

Walk through the difference. The first version costs one line and tells the recruiter nothing —
worse, per Jobberman, it plants a line recruiters actively dislike. The second names an academic
referee and a professional one (the exact mix NigerianQueries prescribes), gives each a role and
organisation so their relevance is obvious, and provides direct contact details so nothing has
to be requested at all. Tell your referees before you list them — a surprised referee is a bad
referee.

## The other six conventions

References are the headline, but the Nigerian fact base covers the full set. Common in Nigeria:

| Field | Convention | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 1–2 pages | Jobberman: "Keep CV length to a maximum of two pages" |
| Photo | Avoid | MyCVCreator: photos "generally discouraged" |
| Address | City/state only | Jobberman: "leave out your full home address to avoid security risks" |
| Date of birth | Avoid | Nairametrics: "highly irrelevant" except government jobs |
| Grades/GPA | Only if strong | Both sources: include only when it strengthens the application |
| Referees | 2–3, named | Jobberman + NigerianQueries, as above |

On length, MyCVCreator adds nuance: "a Nigerian CV is typically more detailed – 1 page for new
graduates and up to 2–3 pages for experienced professionals" — some guidance allows a third page
for very senior profiles, but the two-page cap is the dominant advice.

On the personal-data fields, the direction of travel is clear and consistent:

> "Avoid including sensitive or irrelevant personal details such as your age, date of birth,
> gender, marital status, religion, state of origin" — MyCVCreator

> "It is 2021 and no recruiter needs to know exactly where you live." — Nairametrics

And the address line has a distinctly Nigerian rationale — Jobberman frames the full home
address as a **security risk**, not merely clutter. City and state are enough.

Two exceptions the sources themselves flag: government and public-sector applications may still
expect a date of birth, and grades cut both ways — Jobberman's own example CV proudly shows
"Graduated First Class (CGPA 4.56)". A First Class or Second Class Upper is worth stating;
anything else is acceptable to omit.

**These are conventions, not rules. If the job posting or the employer's application portal says
otherwise, the posting wins — check with the target employer before you send.**

## What this page does not know

Honesty about the sourcing. **No Nigerian government or university career-centre source could be
located for these conventions**, despite a targeted search. The fact base rests on Jobberman (a
major local platform, used as corroboration rather than sole source wherever possible),
MyCVCreator's Nigeria guide, Nairametrics (an established Nigerian business publication), and
NigerianQueries. That is a reasonable base — but it is platform and publisher guidance, not
official guidance.

Two fields are thinner than the rest: the photo advice is effectively single-source
(Jobberman's guide is silent on photos), and the reference convention carries the documented
conflict described above. And conventions move — the on-request habit MyCVCreator describes may
gain or lose ground. A specific job posting always outranks this page.

## What actually costs you

- Writing "References available on request" for a Nigerian employer — the one line local
  guidance says recruiters dislike.
- Listing referees you have not warned. Direct contact details mean direct calls.
- A full home address — flagged by local sources as a security risk, not a formality.
- Padding to three pages when two carries the same information.
- Date of birth, marital status, religion or state of origin on a private-sector CV —
  increasingly left off, per every source in the base.
- Omitting a First Class or Second Class Upper. A strong grade is one of the few personal
  details Nigerian guidance says to keep.
