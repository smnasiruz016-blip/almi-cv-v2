Ask five authoritative sources how long your CV should be and you get three different answers —
and the strictest "one page always" version comes from none of them. MIT leans one page, Oxford
and Stanford say one or two, and two national governments set a hard ceiling at two. **Meanwhile
the only controlled study anyone has run found recruiters preferred the two-page version 2.3 to
1.**

## The failure

The mistake this page prevents is cutting a CV to one page because "that's the rule" — deleting
the quantified second job, the certification, the project that answered the job description —
and then padding the survivor with filler to make the single page look full. People do this
because they believe the one-page rule is research-backed. It is not. **No empirical study
demonstrating that one-page CVs outperform two-page CVs has ever been found — the rule is
print-era career-office convention.** The opposite failure is real too: a three-page CV sent to
a US federal posting is now not merely unfashionable but unuploadable.

## What the institutions actually say

Read them side by side and the disagreement is the finding.

| Source | Verbatim advice |
|---|---|
| MIT CAPD | "Stick to one page, unless you have extensive experience or an advanced degree." |
| Stanford Career Education | "1 – 2 page" |
| Oxford Careers Service | "Keep it to one or two full pages (academic CVs may be longer)—check if there is specified page limit in the application guidance." |
| USAJOBS (US federal) | "Your resume must be two pages or less and explain why you are qualified for the job." |
| Canada Job Bank | "Limit your resume to two pages" |

Note what happened at USAJOBS. Federal resumes were famously the exception — the long-standing
convention, still echoed by many career offices, was that they run longer and more detailed than
private-sector ones. That convention has been reversed by policy:

> "Federal agencies only accept resumes up to two pages in length to comply with the Merit
> Hiring Plan." … "USAJOBS will not allow you to upload or build resumes longer than two pages."
> — USAJOBS

**So the one venue where "no page limit" was genuinely true now enforces the hardest cap on the
list — and the cap is two pages, not one.** US universities lean one page (MIT explicitly),
governments in the US and Canada set a two-page ceiling, and UK sources treat two pages as
normal. There is no cross-institution consensus on "one page".

## The only study on record

In 2018, ResumeGo ran a simulated hiring study — "Settling the Debate: One or Two Page
Resumes" — in which 482 recruitment professionals (recruiters, hiring managers, HR
professionals and C-suite executives) screened 7,712 resumes. Each one-page resume had a
two-page counterpart representing a candidate with similar work experiences, academic
achievements and skills. The findings, verbatim:

> "recruiters were 2.3 times as likely to prefer two-page resumes over one-page resumes"

> "Two-page resumes scored 21% higher with an average score of 8.6, compared to an average
> score of 7.1 for one-page resumes."

The preference grew with seniority: 1.4 times for entry-level candidates, 2.6 times for
mid-level, 2.9 times for managerial.

Now the caveats, because they matter. ResumeGo is a resume-writing service, so this is vendor
research with an interest in the answer. The hiring was simulated, not real. It was never
peer-reviewed. You will also see its headline inflated — "recruiters are 3x more likely…" takes
the 2.9 managerial subgroup and presents it as the overall figure; the overall figure is 2.3.
**The honest reading is narrow: the one available study does not support "always one page" — it
points the other way, with the usual limits of vendor research.**

## So how do you decide? (coaching, not evidence)

What follows is our coaching judgment, built on the sourced guidance above — not a measured
result.

- **Experience volume.** MIT's own one-page advice carries its escape clause: "unless you have
  extensive experience or an advanced degree." Early career with one or two roles: one full,
  dense page is usually enough. A decade of relevant work: cutting to one page means deleting
  evidence.
- **Sector.** Academic and research applications are a different document entirely. MIT puts
  the academic CV at "2 to 4 pages for a younger professional, 4 to 7 pages for a person with
  more experience", and Oxford notes "academic CVs may be longer". If you are applying for
  teaching, research or fellowships, the two-page debate does not apply to you — see our
  CV-vs-resume page for which document you are actually writing.
- **Country.** Common in the US: one page for early-career industry roles, per MIT. British
  sources treat one or two full pages as standard, per Oxford. Canadian and US federal
  guidance caps at two. "CV" itself means different things in the UK and the US, so check
  which convention your target market uses before applying any of this.
- **Whatever you land on, fill the pages you use.** A page and a half of content is a
  formatting decision, not a length decision — tighten to two full pages or cut to one.

## What is not settled

Nobody has run a large, independent, peer-reviewed experiment on CV length. One vendor study
exists; institutional guidance is convention, not evidence; and the institutions do not agree
with each other. We report the disagreement rather than pretending to resolve it. **And one
instruction outranks every guide on this page, including us: the target employer's.** Oxford
says it plainly — "check if there is specified page limit in the application guidance." If the
posting says one page, it is one page. If it is a US federal posting, the system will enforce
two for you.

## What actually costs you

- Cutting your strongest quantified achievements to obey a "rule" no institution above states
  in its absolute form.
- Padding a thin CV to two pages — length is a budget, not a target.
- Sending three pages to USAJOBS: the upload is refused, per their own help pages.
- Citing "recruiters prefer 3x…" — the real overall figure is 2.3, from one non-peer-reviewed
  vendor study.
- Treating your region's convention as universal — see the country branch above.
- Ignoring a page limit written in the application guidance. That instruction beats every
  career service, every study, and this page.
